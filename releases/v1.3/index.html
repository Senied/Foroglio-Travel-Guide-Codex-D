Two nights in one Ascona hotel mean a shower, pool and no luggage move after Cròsa. Val Verzasca then becomes an easy road-trip day rather than another expedition.

Where is it?

Bergamo lies northeast of Milan in central Lombardy. The city is visibly split between the modern lower town and the fortified upper town. Città Alta occupies the hills behind the lower city; its gateways, walls, cobbled lanes and funicular make it feel like a separate settlement even though it is part of a city of more than 122,000 residents.

What is it famous for?

The headline is the sixteenth-century Venetian defensive system, included in the transnational UNESCO property covering Venetian works of defence. Inside the walls, Piazza Vecchia, Palazzo della Ragione, Santa Maria Maggiore and the Cappella Colleoni form one of northern Italy's densest historic ensembles. The funiculars connect the lower city to Città Alta and then to San Vigilio.

The upper city is not a museum set. Around 2,635 people still lived there at the end of 2025. That small permanent population helps explain why it becomes more intimate after day visitors leave and why a one-night stay is materially better than a two-hour stop.

Where is it?

Foroglio is one of the historic seasonal settlements of Val Bavona, a side valley of Vallemaggia in the municipality of Cevio, Canton Ticino. The road enters the valley at Bignasco and follows the Bavona river toward San Carlo. Foroglio sits roughly nine road kilometres from Cevio at 697 metres, below the hanging Val Calnègia.

How many people live there?

A current permanent-resident figure for Foroglio itself is not published as a separate official municipal statistic. The defensible number is the municipality of Cevio: 1,115 permanent residents in 2024 across a very large territory that includes Cevio, Bignasco, Cavergno and Val Bavona. Foroglio has historically been shaped by seasonal movement rather than a large year-round population. This guide therefore does not manufacture a hamlet-level number.

Why is it special?

The settlement combines granite houses, stone paths and a waterfall dropping roughly 110 metres from the rock wall. Val Bavona is also famous for a striking contradiction: major hydroelectric infrastructure exists in the valley, yet houses outside San Carlo are generally not connected to the electricity grid. Solar panels, gas, small water turbines and other local solutions remain part of daily use.

What is the valley?

Val Calnègia is the hanging valley directly above Foroglio. A steep stone path climbs beside the waterfall, reaches the old bridge at Puntíd and then follows clear water through forest and huge granite blocks toward Gerra and the hamlet of Calnègia. There is no road into this side valley; the change from tourist village to foot-only landscape is immediate.

The most distinctive cultural features are the splüi : shelters and working spaces built under or against immense boulders. They are not decorative ruins. They show how inhabitants adapted stone, limited flat ground and seasonal agriculture into a functional landscape.

Who should choose this instead of Cròsa?

Almost everyone who wants a beautiful but sane day. The lower hike delivers the waterfall climb, river, forest, bridge, settlements and stone culture without the exposed high basin, 1,456 metres of ascent or freezing bivouac. It is the correct fallback in unsettled weather and a complete trip in its own right.

What makes it different?

The Cròsa lakes are not an extension of the village stroll. The route continues beyond lower Val Calnègia, rises through Crosa at 1,508 metres and Motto around 2,000 metres, then reaches two high lakes at 2,116 and 2,153 metres. The official route records 1,456 metres of ascent and five hours one way. With overnight equipment, photography, food and normal breaks, six to seven hours is the honest planning figure.

There is no normal overnight accommodation on the route. The reward is a remote basin, marine-blue alpine water, stars and a sunrise that feels completely disconnected from the lakefront resorts below. The cost is weight, cold, exposure and a much narrower weather window.

Can it be a one-day trip?

Not as the default recommendation. A very strong, fast mountain hiker could complete a long return day, but that is not the romantic or safe version being designed here. For a normal fit couple carrying no overnight gear, the official five-hour ascent still implies a major day. Treat Cròsa as two days or choose lower Val Calnègia.

Non-negotiable condition

If either person has not comfortably completed a 1,000+ metre ascent before, test that capacity on another hike first. Do not discover the answer at 1,700 metres with a tent.

Camping and legal distinction

Camping is prohibited throughout the protected lower floodplain. The general Swiss mountain-bivouac exception does not override municipal, protected-area, wetland, closure or site-specific rules. The high-lake coordinate therefore remains unresolved until Comune di Cevio confirms it in writing. Contact cancelleria@cevio.ch or +41 91 759 00 10. Without that confirmation, the overnight is removed and the lower Val Calnègia loop plus booked lodging becomes the plan.

Where is it?

Ascona lies directly on Lake Maggiore west of Locarno, at one of Switzerland's lowest elevations. The municipality had 5,322 permanent residents in 2024. Its lakefront orientation, palm trees and mild climate create a distinctly southern atmosphere after the granite valleys above.

What is it famous for?

The colourful Piazza Giuseppe Motta promenade, the compact Borgo, galleries, cafés and Monte Verità are the familiar markers. Ascona was named Swiss village of the year 2025, recognition built around its former fishing-village character, Mediterranean visual identity and strong tourism offer.

Why it belongs after Cròsa

The contrast is functional, not decorative. A hotel with a hot shower, indoor pool, sauna, real bed and late breakfast turns the high camp into a memorable centrepiece rather than the start of three days of fatigue. Two nights also let Val Verzasca operate as a luggage-free day trip.

Where is it?

Val Verzasca runs north from the Lake Maggiore plain. The tourism route begins at the Verzasca Dam near Vogorno, continues through Lavertezzo and ends in Sonogno. The modern municipality of Verzasca had only 776 permanent residents in 2024 across a large mountain territory. After municipal mergers, current counts for individual localities such as Lavertezzo and Sonogno are not always published as directly comparable standalone figures.

What is it famous for?

The 220-metre arch dam is the dramatic entrance and is associated internationally with the opening stunt imagery of GoldenEye . Lavertezzo is known for the medieval two-arched Ponte dei Salti, emerald water and polished granite. Sonogno, the last village, preserves stone houses, narrow lanes, the valley museum, old bread oven and Casa della Lana.

What the photographs do not tell you

The Verzasca river can be dangerous despite clear, inviting water. Cold, current, depth changes and submerged rock matter. Follow local signs; never treat the bridge pools as an automatic swimming stop.

Standalone verdict

Excellent. It works from Milan as a long day, but Ascona or Locarno is the natural base. It is also the easiest destination to reach without a car using bus 321.

Weather alternatives

Skip Cròsa when conditions are uncertain; the rest of the journey remains complete.

Fallback A - lower Val Calnègia

Keep Camera Alpina and Foroglio. On Sunday, hike the official lower route to Calnègia and return. The official outing is 8.4 km, around three hours and +466 metres. Use the remaining afternoon for Val Bavona and sleep in the valley. Continue to Ascona on Monday morning.

Fallback B - full comfort

Move to Casa Martinelli in Maggia for Sunday night. This is the right choice when rain makes the stone route unattractive or when one person is tired or unwell. It gives a real room, breakfast, free parking and an easy departure toward Ascona.

Fallback C - formal camping

TCS Camping Gordevio retains the outdoor character legally, with tent pitches, pods and simple bivouac structures. It is lower, accessible and much easier to abandon in bad weather.

Cancellation logic

Reserve the fallback on a refundable rate where possible. Do not cancel it merely because the long-range forecast looks attractive; make the bivouac decision from the high-altitude forecast and current ground conditions roughly 48 hours before departure.

Transport, roads, tolls and parking

Compare the main driving route with realistic public-transport alternatives.

Main driving plan

The full journey is materially better with a car because the Fuori Porta-to-Foroglio leg is 195.0 km / about 2 h 48 min before stops, while rail-and-bus connections are much slower. The car also makes a Cevio supply stop, early Foroglio trail start and luggage transfer to Ascona straightforward.

The Milan-Bergamo leg uses the A4 and currently carries an estimated toll of about €5.50. Swiss motorways require a valid CHF 40 annual vignette, physical or electronic, when you use the motorway network. Buy only from the official portal and verify whether your vehicle already has one.

Do not confuse navigation time with itinerary time. Border traffic, parking searches, Friday Milan traffic and the narrow Val Bavona road justify the larger “plan on” values in the distance matrix.

Val Bavona road conditions

The municipal road reopened on 12 April 2025 after the June 2024 flood, under an emergency regime that allows precautionary closure. The road operator says not to enter or drive the valley in adverse weather. Check the official road status , MeteoSwiss and Alertswiss at 48 hours and again immediately before departure; obey every closure, sign and authority instruction. Retain an offline personal emergency plan . The Comune di Cevio reopening notice documents the continuing caution and weather-alert requirement.

Car-free alternatives

Bergamo: easiest; Milano Centrale to Bergamo is about 50 minutes, then ATB line 1/funicular.

Ascona: train to Locarno, then local bus. Excellent for a day trip.

Val Verzasca: bus 321 from Locarno makes a point-to-point valley day possible.

Foroglio: achievable via Locarno, line 315 toward Bignasco and seasonal line 333. The published 2026 timetable states 1 April-31 October, but exact 27-28 September departures and symbols must be checked in SBB/PostBus; groups must reserve at least two working days ahead.

Cròsa: public transport can reach the trailhead in season, but it is a poor fit for a time-critical overnight hike from Milan.

Bergamo

Reserve hotel parking and leave the car. Città Alta ZTL rules are entry-point and time-specific; a hotel or restaurant booking does not automatically authorise entry. Check the official 2026 map and obtain written arrival instructions/plate handling.

Location guide

Where each place is, its population context and why it matters to the journey.

Bergamo

Where: Lombardy, northeast of Milan. Population: 122,263 in 2025. Famous for: Città Alta, Venetian walls, Piazza Vecchia, Santa Maria Maggiore, Cappella Colleoni and funiculars.

Città Alta

Where: fortified hill district of Bergamo. Population: 2,635 at end-2025. Famous for: the intact relationship between inhabited medieval core and UNESCO-listed fortifications.

Cevio

Where: administrative centre of Vallemaggia municipality that includes Val Bavona. Population: 1,115 in 2024. Famous for: gateway services, supplies and access to multiple remote valleys.

Foroglio

Where: Val Bavona, municipality of Cevio, 697 m. Population: no separate current official count. Famous for: granite settlement, roughly 110 m waterfall and off-grid valley culture.

Val Calnègia

Where: foot-only hanging valley above Foroglio. Population: not a separate permanent municipality. Famous for: river, giant boulders, historic splüi , bridge and seasonal settlements.

Laghi della Cròsa

Where: high basin at 2,116 and 2,153 m above Val Calnègia. Population: none. Famous for: remote blue alpine lakes and a 1,456 m ascent from Foroglio.

Maggia

Where: central Vallemaggia, south of Cevio. Population: 2,714 in 2024. Famous for: river landscape, stone villages, Salto waterfall and practical fallback accommodation.

Ascona

Where: Lake Maggiore west of Locarno. Population: 5,322 in 2024. Famous for: colourful lakefront, Borgo, Mediterranean atmosphere, Monte Verità and 2025 village-of-the-year title.

Val Verzasca

Where: separate valley north of the Lake Maggiore plain. Population: municipality 776 in 2024. Famous for: 220 m dam, emerald river, Ponte dei Salti and Sonogno.

Lavertezzo

Where: middle Val Verzasca. Population: current locality count is not used because municipal structure changed. Famous for: medieval two-arched bridge, granite pools and dangerous-looking-beautiful water.

Sonogno

Where: final road village of Val Verzasca. Population: included in Verzasca municipality. Famous for: preserved stone core, valley museum, bread oven, Casa della Lana and nearby waterfall.
